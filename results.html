How we test

  • We ask free AI models the questions people actually ask.
  • Every model gets the same question, the same day, the same settings.
  • Answers are checked against something written down — a law, an official guideline, a known correct result — not against our opinion.
  • The scoring is automatic. Nobody picks the winner.
  • If a model's provider failed to answer, that is recorded as an outage. It is never counted as a wrong answer, and a model that never replied is left out of the scoreboard rather than shown at zero.

What the first test found

Run of 20 September 2026: three free models, ten consumer-rights questions — four about Argentina, four about the EU.

ModelEU questionsArgentine questions
DeepSeek V4 Flash100%87.5%
Qwen 3.8 27B100%66.7%
GPT-OSS 120B100%41.7%

All three were perfect on EU consumer rights. On Argentine rights they were not — and none of them sounded any less certain while getting it wrong. That is the finding: confidence does not drop when accuracy does.

What this does not prove

This was one run of ten questions. Four Argentine questions is a small sample, and DeepSeek's provider failed to answer three of the ten, so it is scored on the seven it did answer. Free models also change without notice, so a result from one week may not hold the next.

We are testing the AI, not the country and not the law. The law is only the measuring stick — it is written down, so anyone can check the answer.

None of this is legal advice. Before you rely on a deadline or a right, check your own country's consumer authority.
